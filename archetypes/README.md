# Brand Archetypes

Brand archetypes are recognizable personality patterns that brands can use
to communicate their identity, values, and relationship with their audience.

This section explores different brand archetypes and demonstrates how each
archetype can be combined with different design styles and Cialdini
persuasion principles.

Each archetype page includes:

- An explanation of the archetype
- Audience motivations
- Visual and verbal cues
- A real-world brand example
- Four original hero treatments
- A modernist design style
- A postmodernist design style
- Two persuasion principles
- Analysis and comparison of the four treatments

---

## Archetypes

### Explorer

**Themes:** Freedom, independence, adventure, and discovery.

The Explorer encourages audiences to leave the familiar behind, discover new
experiences, and create their own path.

[View the Explorer Archetype](explorer.md)

---

### Innocent

**Themes:** Optimism, simplicity, honesty, safety, and happiness.

The Innocent appeals to audiences seeking trustworthy, positive, and
uncomplicated experiences.

[View the Innocent Archetype](innocent.md)

---

### Hero

**Themes:** Courage, achievement, determination, strength, and improvement.

The Hero encourages audiences to overcome challenges, pursue goals, and
become stronger or more capable.

[View the Hero Archetype](hero.md)

---

## Hero Treatment Overview

Each archetype includes four hero treatments created by combining one
modernist design style, one postmodernist design style, and two persuasion
principles.

| Archetype | Modernist Style | Postmodernist Style | Principle A | Principle B |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Explorer | Swiss / International Style | Grunge | Scarcity | Social Proof |
| Innocent | Bauhaus | Pop Art | Authority | Liking |
| Hero | Constructivism | New Wave | Commitment and Consistency | Unity |
